Walk into any new-construction sales office in North Atlanta and you'll be greeted by someone friendly, knowledgeable, and genuinely helpful. Here's what the brochure doesn't say: that person works for the builder. Their job is to sell you that builder's homes on that builder's terms.

## What buyers get wrong

The biggest misunderstanding in new construction is thinking you don't need your own agent because "the price is the price." Builder pricing may be firm, but almost everything else is negotiable: closing-cost contributions, rate buydowns through the builder's lender, upgrade credits, lot premiums, and contract contingencies. Unrepresented buyers routinely leave thousands of dollars of those on the table.

## What your own agent changes

Your agent registers with the builder on day one (often required at the first visit — don't tour without doing this), then negotiates incentives, reads the builder's contract — which is written by the builder's attorneys, for the builder — and pushes for independent inspections at pre-drywall and before closing. Yes, even brand-new homes fail inspections. I've seen it firsthand.

## And it costs you nothing

Builder-paid commissions mean your representation typically comes at no cost to you. There is no version of this where going alone is the savvy move.
