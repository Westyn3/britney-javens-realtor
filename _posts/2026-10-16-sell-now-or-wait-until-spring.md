"We'll probably wait until spring." I hear it every October — and sometimes it's the right call. But not as often as you'd think. Here's how I actually walk sellers through the timing decision.

## The case for listing now

Winter buyers are serious buyers. Nobody tours homes the week before Thanksgiving for fun — they're relocating, their lease is ending, or a life change won't wait for April. Meanwhile, your competition largely leaves the market: inventory in North Atlanta typically bottoms out from Thanksgiving through January. A well-presented home can stand alone in its price range in a way it never could in May.

## The case for waiting

If your home needs meaningful prep — flooring, paint, landscaping that needs a growing season — the months until spring are an asset, not a delay. Rushing a half-ready home to market in November is worse than either option done well.

## The question that actually decides it

It's not "when is the market best?" It's "when will *your* home show best, and what does your timeline require?" A relocation with a start date answers itself. A move-up buyer who needs top dollar and has prep work to do might genuinely be a March listing.
