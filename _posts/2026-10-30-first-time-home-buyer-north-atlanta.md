Buying your first home is one of those things everyone assumes you somehow already know how to do. You don't — nobody does the first time. Here's the honest starting sequence I walk every first-time buyer through.

## Step one: talk to a lender before you scroll

Not to commit — to learn. A 30-minute pre-approval conversation tells you your real budget, your likely payment, and what down-payment assistance or loan programs you may qualify for. It costs nothing and turns anxiety into a number.

## Step two: define the life, then the house

Commute, school zones if kids are in the picture, how you spend weekends. In North Atlanta, that decides more than square footage does — a Norcross townhome, a Roswell swim/tennis neighborhood, and a Cumming new build are three completely different lives at overlapping prices.

## Step three: your agent is free — use one

For nearly all buyers, the agent representing you is compensated through the transaction. That means offer strategy, inspection guidance, and negotiation experience are available to you at no out-of-pocket cost. Going into your first purchase unrepresented saves you nothing and risks a lot.

## Step four: expect the wobble

Every first-time buyer has a moment of "are we crazy?" — usually right after going under contract. It's normal. A good agent's job is to make sure the numbers were sane before that moment arrives.
