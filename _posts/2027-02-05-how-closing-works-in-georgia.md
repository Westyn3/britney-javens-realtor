If you've bought a home in another state, Georgia may surprise you. Here, real estate closings are conducted by attorneys, and that changes who you work with and how the final weeks unfold. Here's the process, step by step.

## Step 1: The contract

Once your offer is accepted, you and the seller sign a purchase contract. It sets the price, the timeline, and the conditions — including your due diligence period, financing terms, and closing date. Earnest money is deposited and held according to the contract.

## Step 2: Due diligence and inspections

This is your window to investigate the property. Schedule your inspection quickly — good inspectors book out. Review the seller's disclosures, and decide what to ask the seller to repair or credit. I come from a family of home builders, so this is the stage where I'm most hands-on, and I'll tell you honestly which findings are cosmetic and which are serious.

## Step 3: Financing and appraisal

Your lender orders the appraisal and finishes underwriting. Avoid big financial moves during this period — no new debt, no job changes without telling your lender first.

## Step 4: The closing attorney does the title work

In Georgia, a closing attorney typically handles the title search, prepares the closing documents, and conducts the closing. The attorney's job is to make sure the title is clear and the documents are in order. Buyers usually choose the attorney, though lenders and builders sometimes have preferences — particularly with new construction, which I cover in [The Builder's Agent Is Not Your Agent](/blog/builders-agent-vs-your-agent/).

## Step 5: The final walk-through

Shortly before closing, walk the property to confirm it's in the agreed condition and that any negotiated repairs were done.

## Step 6: Closing day

You'll typically sign at the attorney's office. Georgia uses a security deed rather than a mortgage or deed of trust, and your closing attorney will explain the documents. Bring a valid ID, and make sure your funds are wired or arranged as instructed.

## Costs to expect

Buyers should plan for lender fees, title and attorney fees, prepaid items like taxes and insurance, and certain Georgia taxes tied to the loan. Sellers typically pay transfer tax and their own closing costs. Exact numbers depend on your loan and price, so ask your lender and closing attorney for an estimate early.

## The wire fraud warning

Wire fraud targeting homebuyers is real. **Never wire money based on an emailed instruction alone.** Call the attorney's office using a phone number you looked up independently, and confirm the instructions verbally before sending anything.

## A few ways to stay ahead

- Respond to your attorney's and lender's requests quickly
- Lock in homeowner's insurance early
- Ask for a draft closing statement a few days before closing
- Keep your agent in the loop
