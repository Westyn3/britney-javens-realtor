Search "best realtor in Alpharetta" and you'll get a wall of ads and directories — all paid placements, none of it proof. Here's a better way: interview agents like you'd interview anyone you're about to trust with your largest asset. These seven questions separate great from average fast.

## 1. How recent are your reviews?

Anyone can have a handful of old five-stars. Look for volume *and* recency — steady reviews from the last year mean the agent is actively closing and clients are happy enough to say so publicly. Read a few; patterns don't lie.

## 2. Who actually shows up for me?

On big teams, the name on the sign isn't always the person at your showings. Solo agents can get stretched thin. Ask directly: who answers my calls, attends my inspections, negotiates my contract? (My answer: The Legacy Team puts two Realtors on every transaction — you get both of us.)

## 3. What does your listing marketing actually include?

Ask for a recent listing and look at it. Phone photos tell you everything. Professional photography, video, 3D tours, and drone coverage should be standard on every listing — not a premium upsell for the million-dollar homes.

## 4. Do you know *my* micro-market?

Alpharetta and Milton aren't one market. A gated golf community off Freemanville, a swim/tennis neighborhood near Webb Bridge, and a downtown Alpharetta townhome shop completely differently. Ask about your specific neighborhood and listen for specifics back.

## 5. Can you evaluate condition, not just price?

Pretty staging hides expensive problems. An agent who understands renovation — what a repair really costs, which flaws are cosmetic and which are red flags — protects you twice: once when you offer, again at inspection. I grew up with contractor parents who flipped homes; I've seen what's behind the drywall.

## 6. How will we communicate?

Ask how fast they typically respond and how they prefer to work — then test it. How an agent treats your first three messages is how they'll treat your transaction.

## 7. What happens when the negotiation gets hard?

Ask for a story: a deal that nearly fell apart and how they saved it — or honestly walked their client away. You want straight talk and data-backed pricing, not a cheerleader.

## How to verify all of it

Read their Google reviews. Click through their recent listings and judge the marketing with your own eyes. Then have a conversation and run this list.
