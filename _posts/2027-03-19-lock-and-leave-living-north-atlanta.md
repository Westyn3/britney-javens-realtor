"Lock-and-leave" is a popular search for a reason: downsizers, frequent travelers, and busy professionals want a home they can close up and walk away from. But the phrase covers several very different property types. Here's how to choose.

## What lock-and-leave really means

It generally means a home where exterior maintenance — landscaping, roofing, sometimes siding and common areas — is handled by an association, so you don't have to. What differs is *how much* is covered and how ownership is structured.

## Condos

In a condo, you typically own the interior of your unit and share ownership of common areas. Association dues are usually higher, because they often cover more: building exteriors, shared amenities, and sometimes certain utilities. Condos can be found around [Buckhead](/blog/buckhead-condos-townhomes-guide/), [Brookhaven](/blog/brookhaven-neighborhood-guide/), and [Vinings](/blog/moving-to-vinings-georgia-guide/).

**Best for:** buyers who want the least possible upkeep and walkable access to dining and shopping.

## Townhomes

Townhomes usually offer more space and a bit more privacy, often with multiple levels and an attached garage. Depending on how the community is set up, you may own the land beneath your unit, and the association typically handles exterior and landscaping. New townhome communities are common in areas like Norcross — see my [Norcross guide](/blog/norcross-homes-new-construction-guide/).

**Best for:** buyers who want more room than a condo without a full yard.

## Low-maintenance detached homes

Some communities offer single-family homes with association-maintained yards and exteriors. They're less common but ideal for buyers who want a detached feel without the work.

**Best for:** downsizers who aren't ready to share walls.

## What to check before you buy

1. **What the dues actually cover.** Compare the monthly cost across properties — and know what *isn't* included.
2. **The association's financial health.** Ask about reserves and any planned or past special assessments.
3. **Rental rules.** Many associations restrict rentals, which matters if you travel or may want to rent later.
4. **Financing eligibility.** Some loan types require the community to be approved, so confirm early with your lender.
5. **Pet, parking, and storage rules.** These small details affect daily life.
6. **Security and access.** If you'll be away often, think about packages, access, and neighbors.

## The trade-off to be honest about

You're swapping control for convenience. The association decides things like paint colors and special projects, and the dues rise over time. For many buyers, the trade is well worth it. For others, it isn't. Knowing which you are is the real decision.
