Search "best realtor in Alpharetta" and you'll find rankings, awards, and a lot of agents who rate themselves number one. I'm not going to tell you I'm the best — "best" depends on what you're buying, what you're selling, and how you like to work. What I can do is hand you the scorecard I'd use if I were hiring an agent in Alpharetta tomorrow, and then show you honestly where I stand.

## The Alpharetta scorecard

**1. Neighborhood fluency.** Alpharetta isn't one market; it's a dozen. [Windward](/windward.html) trades on a different clock than a downtown-walkable townhome, and [Echelon](/echelon.html) behaves nothing like the swim/tennis belt I wrote about in [my Alpharetta family guide](/blog/alpharetta-swim-tennis-neighborhoods-schools/). Ask an agent to explain how two neighborhoods near each other price differently. A real answer has specifics. A weak answer has adjectives.

**2. Speed on new listings.** In the fastest-moving parts of the city, the first weekend decides everything — which is why I wrote up [which Alpharetta neighborhoods move fastest](/blog/fastest-selling-neighborhoods-alpharetta/). Ask how quickly an agent can get you into a house the day it lists, not the Saturday after.

**3. A negotiating story, not a slogan.** Ask for a specific deal where the agent saved a client real money or won a contested offer, and *how*. You're listening for process: what they checked, what they offered, why.

**4. The construction eye.** Alpharetta has plenty of homes from every decade. An agent who can read a roofline, a crawlspace, or a renovation permit protects you from expensive surprises. This is where my background shows: I was raised by home builders and trained on renovations, so I tend to notice what a staged listing is hiding.

**5. Reviews with substance.** Stars are table stakes. Read what clients actually say — whether the agent answered the phone, told hard truths, stayed calm when the deal wobbled.

**6. Straight answers.** The best agent in any city will sometimes tell you not to buy the house, not to list yet, or not to chase a price. Notice who's willing to say that.

## How to actually decide

Interview two or three agents. Ask each the six questions above. Compare how they answer — not how they sound.
