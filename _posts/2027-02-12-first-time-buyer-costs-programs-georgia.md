The sticker price is only the beginning. Most first-time buyers are surprised by how many separate costs appear between "offer accepted" and "keys in hand." Here's a clear map, so there are no surprises. (If you haven't yet, start with my [First-Time Home Buyer guide](/blog/first-time-home-buyer-north-atlanta/).)

## 1. The down payment — less than you might think

A 20% down payment is a traditional target, not a requirement. Depending on your loan, buyers can put down far less. Common paths include conventional loans, FHA loans, and VA loans for eligible veterans and service members. Lower down payments usually mean mortgage insurance or other costs, so the right choice depends on your numbers. A lender can compare options for your situation.

## 2. Closing costs

Plan on closing costs in addition to your down payment. They typically include lender fees, appraisal, title and attorney fees, prepaid taxes and insurance, and certain Georgia taxes tied to the loan. Ask your lender for a loan estimate early, and see my [step-by-step on how closing works in Georgia](/blog/how-closing-works-in-georgia/).

## 3. Earnest money and inspection costs

Earnest money is deposited after your offer is accepted and is generally applied toward your purchase if the deal closes. Budget separately for the inspection — and any specialty inspections, depending on the home.

## 4. The ongoing costs people forget

- **Property taxes**, which vary by county and neighborhood
- **Homeowner's insurance**
- **HOA dues**, if applicable, especially in swim/tennis and gated neighborhoods
- **Maintenance** — a common rule of thumb is to set aside money every year for repairs
- **Utilities**, which are often higher in a larger home

## 5. Assistance programs worth asking about

Georgia has programs designed to help eligible buyers, including down payment assistance through state housing programs such as Georgia Dream. Eligibility, income limits, and purchase-price limits apply and can change, so ask a participating lender for current details rather than relying on any blog post — including this one.

## 6. Apply for your homestead exemption

After you buy, Georgia homeowners can typically apply for a homestead exemption that may reduce property taxes on a primary residence. Deadlines are often early in the year — many counties use an April 1 cutoff — so confirm the date with your county.

## A realistic way to plan

1. **Get pre-approved** with a lender and review the loan estimate line by line.
2. **Build a "cash to close" number**, not just a down payment number.
3. **Keep a cushion** after closing — new homes always surprise you.
4. **Ask about assistance programs** before you assume you don't qualify.
5. **Compare neighborhoods by total cost**, including taxes and HOA dues. That comparison can change which area makes the most sense.
