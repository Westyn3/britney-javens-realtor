Living in Norcross

Gwinnett's Historic Heart,
Still Within Reach

Norcross is the real thing: an 1870s railroad town whose historic downtown never got flattened, modernized, or theme-parked. The brick storefronts along South Peachtree Street hold restaurants, shops, and taprooms; Lillian Webb Park hosts concerts and movie nights on the lawn; and the surrounding historic district's Victorian and craftsman homes give the city a character most metro suburbs can only imitate.

Beyond the historic core, Norcross offers some of the inner metro's most attainable housing — established ranch and split-level neighborhoods, townhome communities, and steady renovation activity as buyers priced out of Brookhaven, Chamblee, and Peachtree Corners discover what a Norcross address actually costs. The West Norcross and downtown-adjacent streets in particular have become quiet appreciation stories.

Location does real work here: I-85 and Peachtree Industrial put Perimeter and Gwinnett job centers in easy reach, Peachtree Corners' tech corridor sits next door, and the international dining of Buford Highway and Jimmy Carter Boulevard surrounds the city. For buyers who want charm, convenience, and a mortgage that leaves room to live, Norcross is one of the metro's best-kept open secrets.

Good to Know

Norcross, GA
Buyer Questions

How much do homes cost in Norcross?

Among the most attainable inner-metro markets: townhomes from the $200s–$400s, established single-family homes largely $350s–$600s, and historic-district homes from the $500s into the $900s. Reach out for a current read.

What is downtown Norcross like?

A preserved 1870s railroad district — brick storefronts, restaurants and taprooms, Lillian Webb Park's concert lawn, and porch-lined historic streets around it. It's the charm people expect to cost twice as much.

Is Norcross a good place for first-time buyers?

One of the best in the inner metro — attainable prices, a real downtown, easy I-85 and Peachtree Industrial access, and appreciation fed by buyers priced out of the neighbors. It's the classic get-in-early market.

Is Norcross a good place to live?

Historic charm, a real community calendar, the metro's best international dining nearby, and honest affordability with quick job-center access. The trade-off is that it's still spreading out from the historic core — which is also the opportunity.
