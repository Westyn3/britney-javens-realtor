Living in Sandy Springs

The City Between
the River & the Perimeter

Sandy Springs is one of Georgia's largest cities, and one of its most quietly livable. Since incorporating in 2005, it has built itself a real downtown at City Springs — a performing arts center, city green, restaurants, and events — while keeping the leafy, established neighborhoods that made people settle here decades ago.

Geography is the superpower. Sandy Springs sits between the Chattahoochee River's national recreation areas — with riverside parks, trails, and put-ins woven through its neighborhoods — and the Perimeter's employment engine, home to major hospitals like Northside and Emory Saint Joseph's plus a deep bench of corporate headquarters. For professionals who work the Perimeter or Buckhead, the commute math is unbeatable.

Housing spans classic mid-century ranches on big wooded lots, renovated traditionals inside the perimeter's edge, luxury townhome and condo development near City Springs and the Perimeter, and riverfront pockets that feel like a mountain retreat ten minutes from a MARTA station. Value varies sharply by school zone and micro-location, so knowing the streets matters here more than most places.

Good to Know

Sandy Springs, GA
Buyer Questions

How much do homes cost in Sandy Springs?

Condos and townhomes from the $300s–$500s, established single-family homes largely $600s–$1.2M, and riverfront and luxury builds beyond $1.5M. Micro-location moves the number a lot here — reach out for a current read.

What is City Springs?

The city's purpose-built downtown — performing arts center, city green, restaurants, and a growing ring of townhomes and condos. It gave Sandy Springs the walkable center it never had, and the housing around it is in demand.

What schools serve Sandy Springs?

Fulton County Schools — including North Springs and Riverwood International Charter — plus easy reach to several of Atlanta's top private schools. Zoning varies block by block; I'm happy to check any address.

Is Sandy Springs a good place to live?

River parks on one side, Perimeter jobs and hospitals on the other, Buckhead ten minutes away — it's one of the most convenient addresses in the metro. The trade-off is Roswell Road traffic; the reward is everything within reach.
